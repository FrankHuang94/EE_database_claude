# Problems 04-02 · Charge, Coulomb's Law and Electric Fields

Chapter: [04-02](../chapters/04-02-charge-coulomb-and-electric-fields.md) · Worked solutions: [04-02 solutions](../solutions/04-02-solutions.md)

**Tiers:** **[A]** concept/recall · **[B]** standard exam level · **[C]** challenge/design.
**Conventions:** $e = 1.602176634\times10^{-19}$ C; $\varepsilon_0 = 8.8541878188\times10^{-12}$ F/m and $k_e = 1/(4\pi\varepsilon_0)$; $m_e = 9.1093837139\times10^{-31}$ kg, $m_p = 1.67262192595\times10^{-27}$ kg; $k = 1.380649\times10^{-23}$ J/K; 1 u = $1.6605\times10^{-27}$ kg. Charges are in vacuum (or air, which is close) unless stated. Answers to 4 significant figures unless exact.

Suggested approach: do all [A] and [B] problems before looking at any solution. Attempt each [C] problem for at least 20 minutes before reading its solution.

## Problems

### Problem 04-02.1 [A]

**Counting charge.** (a) How many excess electrons carry −1 µC? (b) How many electrons pass a point when 1 mA flows for 1 s? (c) Find the charge-to-mass ratio of a proton and of an electron.

### Problem 04-02.2 [A]

**Coulomb's law.** (a) Find the force between two 5 nC charges 2 cm apart. Is it attractive or repulsive? (b) At what separation would the force be 1 µN?

### Problem 04-02.3 [A]

**A point charge's field.** (a) Find the field 3 cm from a 4 nC charge. (b) Find the force on an electron placed there, and its acceleration. (c) Which way does the electron accelerate?

### Problem 04-02.4 [B]

**Finding a null.** Charges of +2 nC at $x = 0$ and −8 nC at $x = 6$ cm lie on the $x$-axis. Where on the axis (apart from infinity) is the field zero? Explain why there is no null between the charges.

### Problem 04-02.5 [B]

**A square of charges.** Four +1 nC charges sit at the corners of a square of side 2 cm. (a) Find the field at the center. (b) Find the magnitude and direction of the net force on one corner charge.

### Problem 04-02.6 [B]

**A ring.** A ring of radius 5 cm carries 10 nC. (a) Find the field on the axis 5 cm from the center. (b) Find where on the axis the field is largest, and its maximum value, by maximizing $E_z(z)$. (c) What is the field at the center, and why?

### Problem 04-02.7 [B]

**Is it a sheet?** A 10 cm × 10 cm plate carries a uniform 50 nC/m². (a) Estimate the field 1 mm from its center. (b) Why is the infinite-sheet formula justified there, and roughly how far away would you stop trusting it?

### Problem 04-02.8 [B]

**Plates for a given field.** (a) What surface charge density on two parallel plates (±σ) produces 1 MV/m between them? (b) How much charge sits on each 1 cm² plate?

### Problem 04-02.9 [B]

**Molecular dipoles against heat.** A molecule with dipole moment $3\times10^{-30}$ C·m sits in a field of $10^7$ V/m (a very strong field). (a) Find the maximum torque and the energy difference between the aligned and anti-aligned orientations. (b) Compare that energy with $kT$ at 300 K. (c) What does the ratio say about how well the molecules align?

### Problem 04-02.10 [B]

**Dipole approximations up close.** A dipole of ±1 nC with 1 mm spacing is observed at $r$ = 5 mm from its center. (a) Find the exact on-axis field and the $2k_ep/r^3$ approximation, and the relative error. (b) Do the same on the perpendicular bisector, where the exact field is $k_ep/(r^2 + d^2/4)^{3/2}$. (c) Which approximation is better here, and why?

### Problem 04-02.11 [B]

**An electrostatic deflector.** Electrons accelerated from rest through 2 kV enter, midway and parallel, between deflection plates 2 cm long and 5 mm apart with 50 V across them. (a) Find their speed and $v/c$. (b) Find the time in the plates, the sideways deflection at the exit and the exit angle. (c) Show that the exit angle is $V_dL/(2V_ad)$, independent of the particle's charge and mass, and check it.

### Problem 04-02.12 [B]

**Ion implantation.** A singly charged boron ion (mass 11 u) is accelerated from rest through 10 kV. (a) Find its kinetic energy in keV and joules. (b) Find its speed. (c) Why do implanters quote energy rather than voltage when the ion may be doubly charged?

### Problem 04-02.13 [C]

**A finite rod: when is it infinite?** A 10 cm rod carries a uniform 2 nC/m. (a) Find the field on its perpendicular bisector at 1 mm, 5 cm and 1 m, and compare each with the infinite-line and point-charge formulas. (b) At what distance is the infinite-line formula 10% too high?

### Problem 04-02.14 [C]

**The disk's two limits.** (a) Starting from the on-axis disk field, show that for $z \gg R$ it becomes the field of a point charge $Q = \sigma\pi R^2$. (Use $(1 + x)^{-1/2} \approx 1 - x/2$.) (b) At what $z/R$ is the disk's field 1% below the infinite-sheet value?

### Problem 04-02.15 [C]

**The field in a gate oxide.** A transistor gate sits on 1.2 nm of SiO₂ (relative permittivity 3.9) with 1.0 V across it. (a) Find the field in MV/cm and compare it with SiO₂'s breakdown field of about 10 MV/cm. (b) What voltage would reach 10 MV/cm? (c) The charge on the gate is $\sigma = \varepsilon_r\varepsilon_0E$. Find it in C/m², and in electrons per nm². What does the answer say about the granularity of charge in small transistors?

### Problem 04-02.16 [C]

**Component against system ESD.** Compare the human-body model (100 pF, 1.5 kΩ) with the IEC 61000-4-2 system-level network (150 pF, 330 Ω) at 8 kV: find for each the stored charge, the stored energy, the idealized peak current and the time constant. Why can a chip that passes 8 kV HBM fail an 8 kV system-level test at a connector?
