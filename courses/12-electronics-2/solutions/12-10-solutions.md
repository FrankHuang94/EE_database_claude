## Solutions

### Solution 12-10.1

$\sqrt{4kT \times 10\ \text{k}\Omega}$ = **12.87** nV/√Hz at 300 K and **6.521** nV/√Hz at 77 K: cooling by a factor of 3.9 lowers the density by its square root.

### Solution 12-10.2

In series, 4 kΩ: **8.141** nV/√Hz (the two noise powers add). In parallel, 750 Ω: **3.525** nV/√Hz. In both cases the result is the thermal noise of the combined resistance.

### Solution 12-10.3

$\sqrt{2q \times 10\ \text{nA}}$ = **56.61** fA/√Hz, and over 1 kHz, $56.61\ \text{fA} \times \sqrt{1000}$ = **1.790** pA RMS.

### Solution 12-10.4

$\sqrt{kT/10\ \text{pF}}$ = **20.35** µV. For 10 µV, $C = kT/(10\ \mu\text{V})^2$ = **41.42** pF.

### Solution 12-10.5

Flicker: $(10\ \text{nV})^2 \times 1\ \text{kHz} \times \ln(100)$ gives **678.6** nV. White: $(10\ \text{nV})^2 \times 9.9\ \text{Hz}$ gives **31.46** nV. Total: **679.3** nV. Below the corner the white floor is irrelevant; this is why precision DC amplifiers use chopping.

### Solution 12-10.6

$g_m = 100\ \mu\text{A}/25.852\ \text{mV}$ = **3.868** mA/V, so $R_{eq} = 1/(2g_m)$ = **129.3** Ω and $e_n = \sqrt{4kTR_{eq}}$ = **1.463** nV/√Hz. $I_B$ = 1 µA, so $i_n = \sqrt{2qI_B}$ = **0.5661** pA/√Hz.

### Solution 12-10.7

$R_{eq} = (2\ \text{nV})^2/(4kT)$ = **241.4** Ω, so $g_m = \gamma/R_{eq}$ = **2.761** mA/V and $I_D = g_mV_{ov}/2$ = **276.1** µA.

### Solution 12-10.8

$\sqrt{2^2 + (10/10)^2}$ = **2.236** nV/√Hz. With a gain of 3: $\sqrt{4 + (10/3)^2}$ = **3.887** nV/√Hz: a low first-stage gain lets the second stage matter.

### Solution 12-10.9

At $T_0$ = 290 K, $\sqrt{4kT_0 \times 10\ \text{k}\Omega}$ = **12.655** nV/√Hz; $i_nR_s$ = **20** nV/√Hz; total $\sqrt{5^2 + 12.655^2 + 20^2}$ = **24.19** nV/√Hz, so NF $= 20\log_{10}(24.19/12.655)$ = **5.627** dB. The optimum is $R_{opt} = e_n/i_n$ = **2.5** kΩ, where $F_{min} = 1 + e_ni_n/(2kT_0)$ = **2.249**, or NF = **3.519** dB.

### Solution 12-10.10

LSB = 2 V/2¹⁴ = **122.1** µV; quantization noise LSB/√12 = **35.24** µV; $C = kT/(35.24\ \mu\text{V})^2$ = **3.336** pF.

### Solution 12-10.11

$R_{eq} = (1.5\ \text{nV})^2/(4kT)$ = **135.8** Ω. The transistor gets 90%: $\gamma/g_m = 0.9R_{eq}$, so $g_m$ = **5.454** mA/V. The load gets 10%: $1/(g_m^2R_D) = 0.1R_{eq}$, so $R_D$ = **2.475** kΩ, and the gain is $g_mR_D$ = **13.5**.

### Solution 12-10.12

$\int_0^\infty df/(1 + (f/f_c)^2) = (\pi/2)f_c$ = **15.71** kHz, so the RMS output is $4\ \text{nV} \times \sqrt{15\,708}$ = **501.3** nV. The noise bandwidth is larger because a first-order filter rolls off slowly: noise above the corner, attenuated but not removed, still adds power.

### Solution 12-10.13

*OTA.* $\overline{v_{n,in}^2} = (8kT\gamma/g_{m1})(1 + g_{m3}/g_{m1})$, so the equivalent noise resistance is $(2\gamma/g_{m1})(1 + 0.5) = 1333\ \Omega \times 1.5$ = **2000** Ω and the density $\sqrt{4kT \times 2000\ \Omega}$ = **5.756** nV/√Hz.

*Folded cascode.* At each folding node the source's noise current $4kT\gamma g_{m,src}$ adds to the input device's, and the mirror adds its own as in the OTA; the cascodes, degenerated by the folding node, add little. The bracket becomes $1 + (1 + 0.5)/1$ = 2.5, so $R_{eq}$ = **3333** Ω and the density **7.431** nV/√Hz, a factor **1.291** higher (1.667 in power). To make the folded cascode as quiet, the folding sources need a low $g_m$, that is a large overdrive, which costs headroom.

### Solution 12-10.14

(a) $R_1 \parallel R_2$ = 900 Ω. The input-referred PSD is $4kT(1\ \text{k}\Omega) + (10\ \text{nV})^2 + 4kT(900\ \Omega)$ = 1.657 × 10⁻¹⁷ + 10⁻¹⁶ + 1.491 × 10⁻¹⁷ V²/Hz, a density of **11.47** nV/√Hz. The noise gain is 10, so the output density is **114.7** nV/√Hz and over 20 kHz the output carries $114.7\ \text{nV} \times \sqrt{20\,000}$ = **16.22** µV RMS.

(b) SNR $= 20\log_{10}(1\ \text{V}/16.22\ \mu\text{V})$ = **95.80** dB.

(c) With $R_1 \parallel R_2$ = 9 kΩ, the resistor term grows to 1.491 × 10⁻¹⁶ V²/Hz and the input density to **16.30** nV/√Hz: the SNR falls by $20\log_{10}(16.30/11.47)$ = **3.05** dB, for no change in gain. Scaling the feedback network up to save current costs noise.

### Solution 12-10.15

(a) $R = e_n^2/(4kT) = (16\ \text{nV})^2/(1.657 \times 10^{-20}\ \text{J})$ = **15.45** kΩ.

(b) $1.5\ \mu\text{V}/6.6$ = **0.2273** µV RMS.

(c) The 1/f part integrates to $S_wf_c\ln(10/0.1) = (9.092\ \text{nV})^2 \times 2100 \times \ln100$ and the white part to $S_w \times 9.9$ Hz; together **0.8946** µV RMS, almost all of it 1/f. The convention would quote $6.6 \times 0.8946$ = **5.904** µV peak-to-peak. (This extrapolates a two-point model three decades below its data; a real part's low-frequency spectrum must be read from its data sheet.)
