# Lab 21-10 · Antennas and Arrays: Patches, the Chu Limit, Squint, Quantization and Array Sizing

Chapter: [21-10 · Antennas, Arrays, Beamforming and mmWave](../../chapters/21-10-antennas-arrays-mmwave.md)

## Objective

- Design patches at 28 and 39 GHz and see how the Chu limit squeezes the bandwidth of small antennas.
- Find grating lobes and scan loss in array patterns.
- Watch a phase-shifter beam squint with frequency, and confirm in ngspice that true-time delay does not.
- Measure the gain loss and sidelobe floor of quantized phase shifters by Monte Carlo and compare with the formula.
- Size a transmit array for minimum DC power and compare analog, hybrid and digital beamforming power.

## Background

`antenna.py` holds the patch equations (`patch_design`), the small-antenna bound (`chu_q`, `bandwidth_from_q`), array factors (`array_factor` with phase-shifter or true-time-delay steering, `planar_af`, `hpbw_deg`, `max_scan_no_grating_deg`), squint (`squint_angle_deg`, `squint_bandwidth`), quantization (`quantization_gain_loss_db`, `quantization_rms_deg`, `quantization_monte_carlo`), array power (`eirp_dbm`, `dc_power_w`, `optimal_elements`, `adc_power_w`) and link helpers (`fspl_db`, `noise_floor_dbm`).

`squint.cir` (generated by `gen_squint.py`) builds the 16-element receive array of Example 21-10.4 twice from matched 50 Ω delay lines: once with fixed phase shifters set at 28 GHz (placed, by linearity, as the phases of the element sources) and once with true-time-delay lines. Voltage-controlled current sources add the 16 outputs, and `.meas` reports the phase-shifter array's loss relative to the TTD array at 26, 28 and 30 GHz, and the TTD array's coherent gain.

## Procedure

1. **Patch and Chu.** *Predict* how much shorter the 39 GHz patch is than the 28 GHz one, and how the Chu bandwidth bound changes as a Bluetooth antenna's sphere shrinks from 10 to 7.5 to 5 mm (Part 1).
2. **Grating lobes and scan loss.** *Predict* where a 16-element array with 0.8λ spacing, steered to 30°, puts its grating lobe; then plot it beside a λ/2 array and tabulate scan loss and beamwidth (Part 2).
3. **Squint.** *Predict* the beam direction at 26 and 30 GHz for the 16-element array steered to 60° at 28 GHz, and the loss toward 60°. Run the patterns and `ngspice -b squint.cir` (Part 3).
4. **Quantization.** *Predict* the gain loss and floor for 3-bit and 4-bit phase shifters on 64 elements, then run the Monte Carlo (Part 4).
5. **Sizing.** *Predict* the DC-optimal element count for 60 dBm EIRP, then sweep it and compare the beamforming architectures (Part 5).

## Expected results

- **Part 1.** The 28 GHz patch is 3.785 × 2.986 mm; the 39 GHz patch is 2.562 × 1.977 mm. The bandwidth bound falls from 7.51% (10 mm) to **3.48** % (7.5 mm) and 1.11% (5 mm): the band needs 3.42%, so the 7.5 mm antenna is just able to cover it at full efficiency.
- **Part 2.** The grating lobe appears at **−48.6**°, at full strength; a 0.8λ array is grating-lobe free only to **14.5**°. At 30°, 45° and 60° the cosine element costs **0.62**, 1.51 and 3.01 dB, and the 16-element beamwidth widens from 6.36° to **7.35**°, 9.02° and 12.99°.
- **Part 3.** The beam moves to 68.85° at 26 GHz and 53.93° at 30 GHz, losing 3.82 dB toward 60° at both; ngspice gives −3.82 dB for the phase shifters, 0 dB at 28 GHz, and 24.08 dB of coherent gain for the TTD array at every frequency.
- **Part 4.** The seeded Monte Carlo gives **0.221** dB of gain loss and a **−31.04** dB floor at the first null for 3 bits, matching $S + (1 - S)/N$ and $(1 - S)/N$; 4 bits give 0.055 dB and about −37 dB. The lab's gain loss is the mean power toward the beam, $S + (1 - S)/N$, which includes the scattered floor; Example 21-10.5 quotes $S$ alone, so its 4-bit figure is 0.056 dB. With 64 elements the two differ by less than 0.01 dB.
- **Part 5.** N* = 145 at 29.04 W; 64 and 256 elements cost 39.34 and 33.84 W, with PA outputs of 18.9 and 6.8 dBm. The digital, hybrid and analog arrays draw 3.22, 1.16 and 1.01 W.

## Discussion

1. **Why does the squint vanish at broadside?** Every element's delay is zero when $\theta_0 = 0$, so a phase shifter set to zero is also a correct true-time delay at every frequency.
2. **Why does the quantization floor fall as 1/N?** The scattered power is the incoherent sum of N small errors, while the beam is their coherent sum, N² times one element's power.
3. **Why is the DC-power optimum flat?** Near $N^*$ the PA term falls exactly as fast as the overhead term rises; the total changes only to second order.

## Optional hardware

A 2.4 GHz patch on FR-4 is easy to make by hand: cut copper tape to the dimensions `patch_design(2.44e9, 4.4, 1.6e-3)` gives, feed it with an SMA at the inset point, and measure $S_{11}$ with a low-cost VNA. The resonance will be a few per cent off (FR-4's permittivity varies), which is why production antennas are trimmed with simulation and measurement.
