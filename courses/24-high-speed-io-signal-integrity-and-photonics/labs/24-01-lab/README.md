# Lab 24-01 · Loss, Stubs and Crosstalk

**Objective.** Confirm the chapter's loss models against an exact lossy-line solution, see what loss does to a single pulse, find a via stub's notch in ngspice, and check the weak-coupling crosstalk formulas on a coupled-line ladder.

**Chapter:** [24-01 · Signal Integrity Fundamentals](../../chapters/24-01-signal-integrity-fundamentals.md)

## Background

§3.2 and §3.3 gave first-order conductor and dielectric loss per unit length; `si.py` also builds the exact ABCD matrix of a uniform line with $R'(f) = R_s/w$ and $G'(f) = \omega C'\tan\delta$, so the closed forms can be checked against $\lvert S_{21}\rvert$. §3.5 predicted a quarter-wave notch at $c/(4\ell\sqrt{\varepsilon_r})$ for a via stub, and §3.6 derived NEXT as a plateau $K_bV_a$ lasting $2T_D$ and FEXT as a pulse of area $(\ell/2v)(C_m/C - L_m/L)V_a$.

## Files

- `si.py`: the chapter's closed forms, an exact lossy line and stub as ABCD matrices, $S_{21}$, and a pulse response by inverse FFT.
- `via_stub.cir`: two through paths with open 50 Ω stubs of 1.6 mm and 0.4 mm in $\varepsilon_r$ = 3.6 (Example 24-01.6).
- `crosstalk.cir`: a 200-section ladder of two coupled 50 Ω lines, 50 mm long, with $C_m/C$ = 0.04 and $L_m/L$ = 0.06 (Example 24-01.7), regenerated by `make_crosstalk.py`.
- `24-01-si-lab.ipynb`: the notebook for all four parts.

## Procedure

**Part 1 · Conductor against dielectric loss** (notebook). Plot the 5-mil, 50 Ω trace's conductor loss and the dielectric loss of the FR-4-class laminate and Megtron 6 from 100 MHz to 60 GHz. *Predict* where FR-4's dielectric loss overtakes the conductor loss.

**Part 2 · A pulse through 10 inches** (notebook). Send a 1 V, 31.25 ps rectangular pulse through 10 inches of each laminate between 50 Ω terminations. *Predict* which keeps more of its height, and roughly how much.

**Part 3 · Via stubs** (ngspice and notebook). *Predict* both notch frequencies, then run `ngspice -b via_stub.cir` and compare with the notebook's ABCD model.

**Part 4 · Crosstalk** (ngspice and notebook). *Predict* the NEXT plateau, its duration and the FEXT area, then run `ngspice -b crosstalk.cir`. Then predict what doubling the rise time to 60 ps does to NEXT, to the FEXT peak and to the FEXT area.

- Part 1: the curves cross at 4.46 GHz. Below that the copper dominates; above it FR-4's dielectric does. Megtron 6's dielectric loss stays below the conductor loss up to 53 GHz.
- Part 2: FR-4 delivers a pulse 0.39 V high, Megtron 6 0.62 V, and the FR-4 pulse arrives later (its $\varepsilon_r$ is higher) and is much wider. The lost height has become a tail that interferes with later bits.
- Part 3: ngspice and the ABCD model agree with the formula to within the sweep's resolution: 24.69 and 98.8 GHz.
- Part 4: ngspice gives a 10.0 mV plateau lasting 578 ps and a FEXT area of −1.155 ps·V, matching the formulas to better than 0.1%. Doubling the rise time leaves NEXT and the FEXT area unchanged and halves the FEXT peak from −38.5 mV to −19.3 mV. The simulated FEXT peak at a 30 ps rise is noticeably larger than the ideal ramp's −38.5 mV, because each lumped section rings at the ramp's sharp corners; the area is immune to that.

## Discussion

1. Why does the exact line's insertion loss agree so closely with $\alpha_c + \alpha_d$?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   At 16 GHz, $R' \ll \omega L'$ and $G' \ll \omega C'$ by factors of hundreds, so the low-loss expansion of $\gamma = \sqrt{(R' + j\omega L')(G' + j\omega C')}$ is accurate to second order in those ratios, and the line is matched to its 50 Ω terminations, so there is no mismatch loss.

   </details>

2. Why is the FEXT area a better check than its peak?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The far-end pulse is the coupling coefficient times the derivative of the aggressor's edge. Its area is the coefficient times the edge's total change, whatever the edge's shape. Its peak depends on the steepest part of the edge, which the ladder's discreteness and any filtering distort.

   </details>

3. What would make the simulated FEXT disappear?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Setting $C_m/C = L_m/L$, as a homogeneous stripline does: the forward capacitive and inductive contributions then cancel exactly. Edit `make_crosstalk.py` to set both ratios to 0.05 and rerun.

   </details>

## Optional hardware
