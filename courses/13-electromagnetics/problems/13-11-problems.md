# Problems 13-11 · Radiation and Antenna Fundamentals

Chapter: [13-11](../chapters/13-11-radiation-and-antennas.md) · Worked solutions: [13-11 solutions](../solutions/13-11-solutions.md)

**Tiers:** **[A]** concept/recall · **[B]** standard exam level · **[C]** challenge/design.
**Conventions:** $c$ = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s; $\eta_0$ = 376.7 Ω; free space; antennas matched and co-polarized unless stated; θ for arrays is measured from broadside.

Suggested approach: do all [A] and [B] problems before looking at any solution. Attempt each [C] problem for at least 20 minutes before reading its solution.

---

## Problems

### Problem 13-11.1 [A]

**Wavelengths and dipoles.** Find the wavelength and the half-wave dipole length at 900 MHz, 2.4 GHz and 28 GHz.

### Problem 13-11.2 [A]

**A very short antenna.** Find the radiation resistance of a 1 cm Hertzian dipole at 100 MHz.

### Problem 13-11.3 [A]

**Decibels.** Express a gain of 100 in dBi, and find the EIRP in dBm and watts of a 20 dBm transmitter with a 15 dBi antenna.

### Problem 13-11.4 [B]

**Far field of a dish.** Where does the far field of a 1 m dish at 12 GHz begin?

### Problem 13-11.5 [B]

**Dish aperture efficiency.** A 1 m diameter dish at 12 GHz has a gain of 40 dBi. Find its effective aperture and its aperture efficiency (effective aperture over physical area).

### Problem 13-11.6 [B]

**A point-to-point link.** A 5.8 GHz link spans 1 km with 1 W transmitted and 20 dBi antennas at both ends. Find the free-space path loss and the received power.

### Problem 13-11.7 [B]

**Range from sensitivity.** A 2.4 GHz receiver needs −90 dBm. The transmitter sends 20 dBm and both antennas have 2 dBi gain. Find the free-space range.

### Problem 13-11.8 [B]

**A lossy dipole.** A half-wave dipole has 2 Ω of conductor and feed loss. Find its radiation efficiency and its gain in dBi.

### Problem 13-11.9 [B]

**Field from power.** A Hertzian dipole radiates 1 W. Find the peak electric field broadside at 1 km.

### Problem 13-11.10 [C]

**Near or far?** For a Hertzian dipole, the ratio of the $1/r^3$ term to the radiation term in $E_\theta$ is $1/(kr)^2$. Find it at $r = \lambda$ and at $r = \lambda/10$, and explain which region a phone held against the head is in at 2 GHz.

### Problem 13-11.11 [C]

**Four elements.** Four isotropic elements are spaced λ/2 apart and fed in phase. Find the directions of the first nulls of the array factor and the directivity.

### Problem 13-11.12 [C]

**Grating lobes.** (a) An array with 0.8λ spacing is steered to 40°. Is there a grating lobe, and where? (b) What is the largest spacing that avoids grating lobes for scans up to ±60°?

### Problem 13-11.13 [B]

**Wi-Fi at 6 GHz.** Under the US indoor 6 GHz rules (5 dBm/MHz EIRP, 30 dBm EIRP maximum), find the maximum EIRP for channels of 20, 80, 160 and 320 MHz.

### Problem 13-11.14 [C]

**Why arrays win twice.** A 64-element array has elements of 5 dBi gain, each driven with 10 dBm, all combining in phase in the beam direction. Find the array gain, the total transmitted power, and the EIRP. Why does EIRP grow as $N^2$?
