# Problems 13-06 · Reflection and Transmission of Waves

Chapter: [13-06](../chapters/13-06-reflection-and-transmission.md) · Worked solutions: [13-06 solutions](../solutions/13-06-solutions.md)

**Tiers:** **[A]** concept/recall · **[B]** standard exam level · **[C]** challenge/design.
**Conventions:** $\eta_0$ = 376.7 Ω; $c$ = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s; media nonmagnetic unless stated, with $n = \sqrt{\varepsilon_r}$; reflection coefficients refer to the electric field, with Ulaby's sign convention for $\Gamma_\parallel$ (chapter §3.4).

Suggested approach: do all [A] and [B] problems before looking at any solution. Attempt each [C] problem for at least 20 minutes before reading its solution.

---

## Problems

### Problem 13-06.1 [A]

**Into a board laminate.** A plane wave in air meets a lossless dielectric with $\varepsilon_r$ = 4 at normal incidence. Find Γ, τ, the reflected and transmitted power percentages, and the standing-wave ratio.

### Problem 13-06.2 [A]

**Into water.** Repeat Problem 13-06.1 for a lossless medium with $\varepsilon_r$ = 81 (an idealization of water at low microwave frequencies).

### Problem 13-06.3 [A]

**In front of a mirror.** A 10 GHz plane wave strikes a perfect conductor at normal incidence. How far apart are the zeros of the electric field, and how far from the conductor is the first maximum?

### Problem 13-06.4 [B]

**Looking up from under water.** Water has $n$ = 1.33. Find the critical angle for light going from water into air, and the angle in air of a ray that meets the surface at 30° from the normal in the water.

### Problem 13-06.5 [B]

**At Brewster.** A wave in air strikes glass ($n$ = 1.5) at the Brewster angle. Find the transmission angle, $\Gamma_\perp$, and the TE reflected power percentage.

### Problem 13-06.6 [B]

**Matching into alumina.** A microwave signal must pass from air into a ceramic with $\varepsilon_r$ = 9 without reflection at 10 GHz. Find the permittivity and the thickness of a quarter-wave matching layer.

### Problem 13-06.7 [C]

**A half-wave radome.** A radome is a slab with $\varepsilon_r$ = 4 in air, designed to be transparent at 10 GHz at normal incidence. Find its thickness, and its power reflectance at 11 GHz and at 12 GHz.

### Problem 13-06.8 [B]

**A fibre's acceptance.** An optical fibre has an illustrative core index of 1.47 and cladding index of 1.46. Find its numerical aperture, its acceptance half-angle in air, and the critical angle at the core–cladding boundary.

### Problem 13-06.9 [B]

**Radio onto the sea.** Seawater at 10 kHz has σ = 4 S/m and $\varepsilon_r$ = 81. Find its intrinsic impedance, the reflection coefficient for a plane wave arriving from air at normal incidence, and the percentage of power transmitted into the sea. Compare with the estimate $4\,\text{Re}\{\eta_c\}/\eta_0$.

### Problem 13-06.10 [B]

**An aluminium shield.** Estimate the plane-wave shielding effectiveness of a 0.5 mm aluminium sheet (σ = 3.5 × 10⁷ S/m) at 1 MHz, giving the absorption and reflection losses separately.

### Problem 13-06.11 [C]

**A Salisbury screen.** A resistive sheet of 376.7 Ω per square is placed a quarter wavelength (at $f_0$) in front of a flat metal plate, in air. (a) Explain, using the transmission-line analogy, why a normally incident wave at $f_0$ is not reflected at all. (b) Find $\lvert\Gamma\rvert$, in linear terms and in dB, and the reflected power percentage at $1.2f_0$.

### Problem 13-06.12 [C]

**The evanescent tail.** Light of 1550 nm wavelength (in vacuum) travels in glass ($n$ = 1.5) and meets a glass–air boundary at 60°. Show that it is totally reflected, and find the decay constant of the evanescent field in the air and the distance over which it falls by $e$.

### Problem 13-06.13 [B]

**Energy is conserved.** For the TE wave of Example 13-06.5 (air to glass at 45°), find $\tau_\perp$ and the transmitted power fraction $T = (\eta_1\cos\theta_t/\eta_2\cos\theta_i)\lvert\tau_\perp\rvert^2$, and check that $R + T$ = 1.

### Problem 13-06.14 [B]

**Reading a standing wave.** A 10 GHz plane wave of 1 V/m peak in air strikes the dielectric of Problem 13-06.1 at normal incidence. Find the largest and smallest total field amplitudes in the air, where the smallest occurs, and the distance from there to the nearest maximum.
